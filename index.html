Central argument

Generative AI reconfigures how learners create, judge, explain, and demonstrate knowledge. When a course grades only the AI-assisted deliverable, it can no longer see what the student actually knows.

  • Sociomateriality. Learning practices emerge through entanglements of people and technology (Orlikowski, 2007; Orlikowski & Scott, 2008).
  • Bloom's Taxonomy. A useful reference point, not a hierarchy we need to declare obsolete.
  • Contribution. A theoretically grounded design framework for authentic assessment.

The title is a working title and may change before submission.

Research questions

  1. Sociomaterial agency. How does generative AI reconfigure human and material agency across creating, evaluating, analyzing, and understanding?
  2. Authentic assessment. How can CEAU translate sociomaterial principles into assessment of judgment, understanding, and competence?

Both questions stay conceptual for this paper. AI fluency in the EMBA program appears only as an illustrative transfer case.

The CEAU model

CEAU reorders where the grade comes from. Students may use AI to get a first working version. Most of the grade then goes to what AI can't do for them: judging that work, explaining it, and changing and defending it on their own.

C

Create / Scaffold

Get a working version with AI.

E

Evaluate

Test its validity and identify flaws.

A

Analyze

Explain the mechanisms and trade-offs.

U

Understand & Refine

Change and defend the work without AI.

C 20%
E + A + U 80%

CEAU appears in the paper as a design case from AI 410, not as an impact study: the course will not produce learner outcome data before the January submission target.

What the paper contains

A conceptual paper, an illustrated CEAU model, and a research agenda. It reports no learner findings.

  1. Problem. AI-generated deliverables obscure what students know.
  2. Theory. Orlikowski and sociomateriality; agency and practice.
  3. Design case. CEAU in AI 410 and the 20/80 assessment logic.
  4. Contribution. Assessment design principles, with an EMBA transfer example.
  5. Next study. A pilot protocol and propositions for research with a student cohort.
