# Beyond Bloom's Pyramid

**Sociomateriality, CEAU, and authentic learning in the age of generative AI**

A research collaboration between **Jim O'Farrell** and **Clark Jason Ngo**,
bridging academic theory on sociomateriality with practical AI-in-the-classroom
design. Kicked off November 2, 2026.

> Working title — provisional until agreed by both collaborators.

## Central argument

Generative AI reconfigures how learners create, judge, explain, and demonstrate
knowledge. Assessment that grades only the AI-assisted deliverable obscures what
students actually know.

- **Sociomateriality** — learning practices emerge through human–technology
  entanglements (Orlikowski).
- **Bloom's Taxonomy** — a useful reference, not a hierarchy we must claim is
  obsolete.
- **Contribution** — a theoretically grounded design framework for authentic
  assessment.

## Research questions

| | Focus | Question |
|---|---|---|
| **RQ1** | Sociomaterial agency | How does generative AI reconfigure human and material agency across creating, evaluating, analyzing, and understanding? |
| **RQ2** | Authentic assessment | How can CEAU translate sociomaterial principles into assessment of judgment, understanding, and competence? |

Both questions stay conceptual for Phase 1. EMBA AI-fluency is limited to an
illustrative transfer case.

## The CEAU model

| Stage | Student activity | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **C** — Create / Scaffold | Get a working version with AI | 20% |
| **E** — Evaluate | Test validity and identify flaws | |
| **A** — Analyze | Explain mechanisms and trade-offs | 80% combined |
| **U** — Understand & Refine | Change and defend the work *without* AI | |

CEAU is presented as a **design case, not an impact study**: AI 410 will not
produce learner outcome data in time for the January submission. See
[`design-case/`](design-case/).
